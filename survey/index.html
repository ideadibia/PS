Understanding medication management

We asked people who take daily medications about what makes them forget to take it. The responses help us better understand the everyday challenges behind medication routines—and where better support may be needed.

Why we asked

Medication routines are more complicated than they look.

Taking medication as prescribed can involve more than remembering a pill. Treatment plans can change, doses may need to fit around meals or other medications, and some routines include injections or topical treatments as well as tablets.

We wanted to understand how people navigate these real-life details, what they already use to stay on track, and where current approaches fall short.

What we're learning

Five challenges that shape medication routines

Themes from survey responses and discovery interviews

01

Medication therapies are not static

They change fairly often, in response to side effects, new symptoms, or even difficulties taking the medication correctly.

02

Some dose times are harder to manage

Most people are very good at taking medications in the morning and evening. Medications outside those windows can be more difficult—especially doses that need to be taken with food or spaced apart to avoid drug interactions.

03

Medication adherence affects people of all ages

Medication adherence is a challenge across age groups for people living with chronic conditions, while many existing solutions have focused on older adults aged 65 and above.

04

People try different tools to stay on track

Many people have tried some combination of a pill organizer, a mobile application, and physical reminders. Blister packs and pill dispensers are less commonly adopted, for a variety of reasons.

05

Support is needed beyond pills

There is a lack of affordable solutions that support adherence for non-oral medications, such as injections or topical applications—especially when they are taken in addition to pills.

Beyond the numbers

Listening to the people behind the routines

Alongside the survey, we spoke with patients, caregivers, pharmacists, physicians, home care agencies, insurance companies, and others who see medication management from different perspectives.

These conversations reinforced an important point: medication routines are personal, they change over time, and a useful solution needs to reflect the full routine—not just whether a dose was taken.

90+ discovery interviews with people across the medication care journey

A note about these findings: These results are exploratory and reflect the experiences of people who chose to participate. They are intended to help us understand medication management challenges, not to represent every person who takes medication or provide medical advice.

Common questions

About the medication management survey

What was the Pillsette survey about?

The survey explored what makes it difficult for people who take daily medications to remember doses and manage their medication routines.

Who took part in the survey?

The public survey results post reported 236 respondents from the United States and Canada.

What did Pillsette learn?

The survey and discovery conversations highlighted changing medication plans, challenges with doses outside morning and evening routines, the limits of existing reminder tools, and the need to support non-pill medications as part of the same routine.
